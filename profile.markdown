I am an assistant professor at the [Center for Integrated Japanese Studies](https://cijs.oii.tohoku.ac.jp/en/people/staffs/) at Tohoku University. My research focuses on the social and economic history of Tokugawa Japan (1600-1868). I am currently working on a book project about merchant houses during the Tokugawa period. The project explores the conditions that both enabled and limited the success of large-scale merchant businesses within a legal and institutional environment that ought to have inhibited both capital formation and the development of merchant power. It draws on the extraordinarily detailed archive of the Nakai Genzaemon, one of the most expansive and wealthy houses of the second half of the Tokugawa period. I have published two articles making use of this archive, one in the Economic History Review, the other in Rekishi. Alongside this work, I have also begun a project on guilds, finance, and the state, making use of the records of the Kotani, a materia medica trader in early 19th century Sendai, held at the Tohoku University Library. I am actively digitizing and publicizing the Kotani records on the [T&#333;hoku University Digital Archives](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/portal/). For more information on my current work, please refer to my [researchmap](https://researchmap.jp/johndamico).

Outside of my research, I am interested in local document preservation and post-disaster recovery in Japan. I have worked with the Miyagi Shiry&#333; Network on the restoration and use of historical documents damaged by the 3/11 triple disaster, and have presented on local history in Japan.
